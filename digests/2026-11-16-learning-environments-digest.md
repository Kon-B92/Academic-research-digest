## AI, Engagement & SDT (Student Psychological Needs)

### ★ Unlocking student engagement with generative AI: need satisfaction as the key, self-efficacy as the gatekeeper
- **Authors:** Yuxuan Xin, Mohd Shahril Nizam Bin Shaharom, Kenny Cheah (taken from the matching SSRN preprint; the Springer author list couldn't be checked directly. See the transparency note.)
- **Source:** *Education and Information Technologies* (Springer)
- **Publication date:** 11 November 2026 (from search snippets; the SSRN preprint and University of Malaya listings carry earlier dates)
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14134-6
- **Keywords matched:** AI in education · student engagement · Self-Determination Theory · learning environments

In an SEM study of 389 undergraduates, students who saw generative AI as offering useful affordances reported higher satisfaction of their autonomy, competence and relatedness needs. That need satisfaction in turn predicted behavioural, cognitive and emotional engagement, with the strongest indirect path to cognitive engagement (β ≈ .39). AI self-efficacy strengthened the link from affordances to need satisfaction only modestly, so the "gatekeeper" in the title may overstate its role. For teaching quality, the authors say design effort should go into the *learning environment around* the AI tool (how it supports students' choice, their sense of mastery and their connection to others), not into the technology alone. The clearest finding is that engagement with AI runs through psychological needs: AI raises engagement only to the extent that it meets them.

### ★ Classroom Social Climate, AI Literacy, and Student Engagement in AI-Mediated English Learning: Linear and Configurational Perspectives
- **Authors:** Not resolved (see the transparency note)
- **Source:** *Behavioral Sciences* (MDPI), Vol. 16, Issue 11, Article 2137
- **Publication date:** 10 November 2026 (from search snippets)
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/16/11/2137
- **Keywords matched:** learning environments (classroom social climate) · AI in education · student engagement

Using survey data from 570 EFL students at one university in eastern China, the study combines structural equation modelling with fuzzy-set QCA (fsQCA). The analysis asks how the classroom's social climate and students' AI literacy, alone and in combination, relate to engagement in AI-mediated language learning. For instructional effectiveness, the configurational design matters: it treats teacher-shaped climate and student AI skill as conditions that combine, rather than competing predictors. That suggests AI integration works best when relational climate is also strong. Read through SDT, classroom social climate is a proxy for relatedness support, and AI literacy maps onto competence. Pairing them makes the case that AI tools do not replace a needs-supportive classroom. *(Cross-sectional design, from a single institution.)*

### ★ From Dialogue to Task: How Cognitive Engagement With Generative Artificial Intelligence Relates to Post-Dialogue Activity and Task Outcomes
- **Authors:** Xiaoyu Zhuang et al. (three further co-authors not surfaced in accessible sources)
- **Source:** arXiv preprint (arXiv:2611.11435). *Not yet peer-reviewed.*
- **Publication date:** about 8 November 2026 ("posted ~8 days ago" per search index; consistent with the 2611 ID)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11435
- **Keywords matched:** AI in education · student engagement (cognitive) · learning environments (task workflow)

The authors code students' dialogues with generative AI using an adapted ICAP framework, which rates engagement from passive to interactive. Most dialogue was passive or active. Higher-engagement dialogue was less often followed by students ending the session, and regression models linked dialogue and workflow features to re-engagement, peer-rated artefact quality and practice performance. For teaching quality, the main lesson is about measurement: counting AI interactions says little. Instructors and researchers should track separately where in the task workflow students consult AI, how deeply they engage, and what they do afterward. From an SDT angle, the gap between "activity after AI" and "quality of outcome" warns against reading AI use as a sign of competence, so designs that require constructive and interactive engagement are more likely to build real mastery.
